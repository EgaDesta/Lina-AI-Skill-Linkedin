# Recommendations

LinkedIn's recommendation feature is the cheapest credibility transfer on the
platform, and the most misused. Two vague ones hurt; three specific ones help.

## Who to ask

Ranked by what the audience already trusts.

1. **Someone the audience knows and you worked with directly.** Best source. The
   audience can check the relationship.
2. **A client with `permission: granted`.** Only with written permission.
3. **A colleague from the same team.** Safe, but weaker: the audience cannot
   verify how closely you worked.
4. **A manager you reported to.** Sometimes, when there is an obvious reason they
   would say something specific. Forced praise from above reads as filler.
5. **Someone you have never worked with.** Useless. Do not ask strangers.

Do not ask people who cannot answer with specifics. A recommendation of "great
teammate" is a public statement that the writer has nothing specific to say.

## The ask

Send the request with the question prefilled. People who ask from scratch get
declined or receive something generic. Give them the material.

```
Hi {name} — would you write a short recommendation for my profile?

A couple of things you actually saw, if any of these ring true:

- {specific thing you did together, with a result if you know it}
- {a decision they watched you make}
- {something they came to you about}

No need for anything formal. Two sentences is more useful to me than a paragraph
that could have been written about anyone.

Thanks either way.
```

## What a useful recommendation contains

- One specific situation
- One thing the person did
- The observable result

```
"{Name} rebuilt our invoice process while we were mid-acquisition and
understaffed. Took it from three people reconciling by hand to one person
checking an exception list. I have never seen a change stick like that one."
```

Not:

```
"{Name} is a hard worker and a great team player. I would recommend him to
anyone looking for a {job title}."
```

The first is specific enough to transfer trust. The second is identical to every
other recommendation and transfers none.

## The system

**Month 1–3.** Ask four people, using the prefilled ask above. Do not send a
naked request.

**Month 3–6.** Read the weak ones. If two of four are vague, the problem is the
ask, not the writers. Re-ask with more specific material.

**Month 6+.** Ask one new person per quarter, from new work. A recommendation
per quarter keeps the profile current. Two a year refreshes it.

## Removal

Remove, without ceremony, any recommendation that is now wrong. A recommendation
that praises a process you have since publicly criticised is a credibility hole
someone will find.

Also remove: recommendations from people whose own profile would make a reader
wonder about your judgement, and recommendations that read as competitor
endorsement if it was not.

## Privacy

Do not paste a recommendation verbatim into a post without asking. That is a
person's words attached to your distribution. Ask, and expect a no more often
than you expect a yes.

## What this does not affect

Recommendations are not scored by `rubric/profile-rubric.yaml`. They are not
measurable, so the rubric does not pretend to score them. `ps3` checks for a
case study in Featured; recommendations are a separate trust surface, and they
are assessed by a human reading the profile.
