# Skill Stack

50 skills maximum. The three pinned ones are what LinkedIn displays. Those three
are the only ones that matter for positioning.

## `score-profile.mjs` `sc3`

The 3 pinned skills must include `keywords.primary.keyword` or a direct variant.
It is a `nice` severity — 3 points — but a pinned skill list that contradicts
the headline is a coherence problem no score captures.

## The three pinned

Pin, in this order:

1. `keywords.primary.keyword` — the one buyers search
2. `positioning.method` — the named approach, if LinkedIn has a matching term
3. The skill the offer actually delivers

Not: the most impressive skill, the longest one, the one with the most
endorsements.

## The rest, 4–10 well chosen

Fill below the pinned three with skills that a buyer in `audience.primary`
searches. Twelve to fifteen is plenty. The remaining slots do nothing and a full
list of generic terms reads as keyword stuffing.

Order matters below the top three too. LinkedIn shows a sample. Put the ones you
want seen in positions 4–7.

## Do not add

- A skill you do not have. `ps4` and `proof.policy` apply to skills as much as
  to posts: a claimed skill invites the question you cannot answer.
- `Microsoft Office`, `Internet`, `Communication` — the platform equivalents of
  listing your height.
- Anything with fewer than three endorsements from people in the target audience
  unless it is directly relevant to the offer.
- `Social Media Management` for a B2B consultant persona. It changes who
  follows you, in the wrong direction.

## Adding skills

Add skills that a specific post will then be written about. The reverse order —
write posts, then add the skills the posts need — produces a more coherent
profile than buying skills first and hoping content follows.

## Endorsements

Ask for them in the same way as recommendations: with the specific thing prefilled.

```
If you worked with me on {specific thing}, would you endorse {skill}? No need if
it isn't what you actually need help with.
```

Which is also correct behaviour: skills you do not need endorsements for should
not be pinned.

## Consistency checks

The skill list, the headline, the About keywords, and the content pillars must
all point at the same specialty. `score-profile.mjs` `sc2` checks a secondary
keyword in the About preview; `pcx2` checks at least two pillars are visible in
the profile copy.

A profile with an automation headline, a marketing About section, and a skill
list split between them reads as two people sharing a login. The score will not
go below a threshold for it, because no single check catches it — but nobody who
lands on it will convert, and the numbers will say so for months before anyone
diagnoses the cause.

## Review cadence

Quarterly. Remove anything not used in the last two years and anything not
supporting the offer. Adding is cheap and meaningless; removing is what makes the
list legible.
